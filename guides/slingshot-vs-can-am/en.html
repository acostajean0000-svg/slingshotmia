Slingshot vs Can-Am Spyder & Ryker

Both have three wheels — and that’s where the similarity ends. One is a low, open sports car with a steering wheel; the other is a motorcycle you straddle. Here’s the honest comparison so you pick the right one.

1

The core difference

both have 3 wheels — that’s where it ends

Slingshot: two wheels front, one rear. Steering wheel, pedals, side-by-side bucket seats, seat belts — you sit in it like a low sports car. Passengers face forward next to you, so conversation and shared experience come naturally.

Can-Am Spyder / Ryker: two wheels front, one rear too — but you straddle it like a motorcycle with handlebars, and the passenger sits behind you. Higher seating position, more upright, more "bike".

Slingshot: 178–203 hp from a 2.0L four-cylinder in a ~1,650 lb chassis — genuinely quick (0–60 in ~4.9 s on the R). Manual 5-speed or AutoDrive paddle automatic.

Can-Am: Ryker uses 600cc or 900cc engines (roughly 50–82 hp) with twist-and-go automatic — approachable rather than fast. Spyder RT models are bigger tourers with 1330cc triples, built for comfort and mileage more than acceleration.

Slingshot: autocycle in nearly every state — regular driver’s license, no motorcycle endorsement (see our License guide). If you can drive a car, you can drive it: steering wheel and pedals feel instantly familiar.

Can-Am: rules vary more by state — some require a motorcycle license or a 3-wheel endorsement. The handlebar controls also carry a small learning curve for non-riders.

Choose the Slingshot if you want a head-turning, car-like open-air sports experience, you’re a driver rather than a rider, or you’re sharing the ride with someone side-by-side. It’s the better show-and-fun machine — and the better rental for tourists.

Choose a Can-Am if you want motorcycle riding position with three-wheel stability, plan long touring days with luggage, or already ride bikes and prefer handlebars. Spyders carry far more storage.

Head to head, honestly

⚡Faster: Slingshot, clearly — 203 hp vs. 50–82 hp on a Ryker
🧳More practical touring: Can-Am Spyder — real storage, weather protection, upright comfort
🪪Easier legally: Slingshot in most states (car license)

There’s no wrong answer — they’re different machines for different moods. But for a Miami Beach cruise with a friend, the side-by-side seating and steering wheel are hard to beat.

Slingshot vs Can-Am — FAQ

What's the difference between a Polaris Slingshot and a Can-Am Spyder?

The Slingshot has a steering wheel, pedals and side-by-side seats — you sit in it like a low sports car. The Can-Am Spyder and Ryker use handlebars and a straddle seat like a motorcycle, with the passenger behind you.

Is a Slingshot faster than a Can-Am?

Yes, considerably. The Slingshot makes 178–203 hp and hits 60 mph in about 4.9 seconds. A Can-Am Ryker makes roughly 50–82 hp; the Spyder RT is built for touring comfort rather than acceleration.

Which is easier to drive, a Slingshot or a Can-Am?

For someone who drives cars but doesn't ride motorcycles, the Slingshot is far more intuitive — steering wheel, pedals and seat belts. Can-Am's handlebar controls take some adjustment.

Do you need a motorcycle license for a Can-Am vs a Slingshot?

In most states the Slingshot is an autocycle needing only a regular driver's license. Can-Am requirements vary more by state and often involve a motorcycle or three-wheel endorsement — check your state.

Which is better for two people?

The Slingshot, for most riders — passengers sit beside you rather than behind, so you can talk, share the view and take photos together. The Spyder wins if you need luggage space for long touring.
