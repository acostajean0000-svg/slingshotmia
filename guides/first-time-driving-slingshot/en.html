First Time Driving a Slingshot?

If you can drive a car, you can drive a Slingshot — steering wheel, pedals, seat belts, no motorcycle license needed. But five things surprise almost every first-timer. Here they are before you turn the key.

EnglishEspañol
1

Before you pull away

60 seconds that make the whole ride better
1Seat & mirrors: you sit low — adjust so you can see past the hood corners and both mirrors before moving
2Buckle up: 3-point belts, always. In Florida no helmet is required (see License guide) but belts are the law
3Sunglasses & sunscreen: no roof means sun and wind for the whole ride — and tie back long hair
4Phone mount + Bluetooth: pair before you roll; you won’t hear notifications over wind at speed
5Know your transmission: manual clutch or AutoDrive paddles — ask at pickup and try it in the lot first
2

The 5 things that surprise everyone

none of them are hard — just different
🔊It’s loud and low. You’re inches from the pavement with no roof. Everything feels twice as fast — 45 mph is plenty on Ocean Drive
🛞One rear wheel drives it. Squeeze the throttle smoothly; stomping it spins the tire, especially turning or on wet pavement
↩️Turns feel different. Three wheels + light weight = take corners slower than instinct says the first few times
🕳️Bumps are real. Low ground clearance — watch speed bumps, steep driveways and potholes
👀Everyone stares. Phones out, thumbs up, questions at every light. Enjoy it — but keep your eyes on the road
3

Your first 10 minutes

a simple warm-up that builds confidence
1Do a lap of the parking lot: feel the steering, brakes and (if manual) the clutch bite point
2Practice one smooth start and one full stop before entering traffic
3Take surface streets first — save the causeway or highway for after you’re comfortable
4Leave extra following distance; braking feel is different from a car
5Then go have fun — try our Miami scenic routes for the best first ride

First Time Driving — FAQ

Is a Polaris Slingshot hard to drive?

No. If you can drive a car you can drive a Slingshot — it has a steering wheel, pedals and seat belts. The differences are that it sits very low, feels much faster, and one rear wheel handles all the power, so be smooth on the throttle.

Do I need any special license or experience?

In Florida and most states, no — a regular driver's license is enough because the Slingshot is classified as an autocycle. No motorcycle endorsement and no prior riding experience needed.

Is it safe for a first-time driver?

Yes, with normal care. It has seat belts, roll hoops, ABS and traction control. Take the first few minutes slowly, avoid hard throttle in turns, and leave extra following distance.

Should I get the manual or automatic?

If you're not confident with a clutch, choose AutoDrive (paddle-shift automatic) — it removes the only tricky part. Ask us which units are available when you book.

What should I wear and bring?

Sunglasses, sunscreen, and hair tied back. There's no roof, so wind and sun are constant. A light jacket helps at night or in rain — see our rain guide.
